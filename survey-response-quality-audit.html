Survey Response Quality Audit

A reproducible survey-quality workflow that uses paradata and answer-pattern checks to identify incomplete responses, speeders, straight-lining, attention-check failures, logical inconsistencies, duplicate-like submissions, invalid values, and low-quality open text.

Overview

This case study audits a simulated online survey for common response-quality problems. The dataset contains 1,250 responses, including 1,200 completed responses and 50 partial responses.

The audit deliberately uses transparent, interpretable rules rather than a black-box quality score. Each respondent receives individual QC flags, followed by a recommendation for manual review or exclusion.

Headline results

272 responses received at least one QC flag
177 responses recommended for manual review
95 responses recommended for exclusion
177.3s data-derived speeder threshold

The speeder threshold is one-third of the median completed-response duration of 532.0 seconds.

Quality checks

Partial responses

Flags surveys not marked complete or with progress below 100%.

Speeding

Flags completed surveys below one-third of the median completed survey duration.

Straight-lining

Detects the same response across all eight items in the main 1–7 rating grid.

Attention-check failure

Flags respondents who do not select the instructed answer on the embedded attention check.

Invalid values

Checks age, NPS, and 1–7 rating variables for values outside their permitted ranges.

Logical inconsistencies

Tests contradictions across eligibility, recency, routing, support-contact, and related survey answers.

Duplicate-like submissions

Identifies repeated browser fingerprints combined with identical substantive answer patterns.

Low-quality open text

Detects blank, nonsensical, extremely short, numeric-only, or repetitive open-ended responses.

A single behavioural warning is not treated as sufficient evidence for automatic exclusion. Partial responses, invalid values, and duplicate-like submissions are hard failures; otherwise, at least two behavioural indicators are required for an exclusion recommendation.

Decision logic

The workflow separates hard failures from behavioural warning signals. Partial responses, invalid values, and duplicate-like submissions are recommended for exclusion immediately. For behavioural indicators such as speeding, straight-lining, attention failure, logic errors, and low-quality open text, one signal leads to review while two or more lead to exclusion.

This approach keeps the decision rule auditable and avoids treating a single suspicious pattern as definitive evidence of poor response quality.

Project files

The analysis is fully reproducible from the public repository. The Python script reads the raw simulated survey data, generates the respondent-level QC flags and summary, creates both figures, and rewrites the Markdown report.
