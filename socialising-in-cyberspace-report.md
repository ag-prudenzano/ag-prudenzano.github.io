# Socialising in Cyberspace: A Phenomenologically Informed Interview Study of Discord Users' Social Experiences

## Project Snapshot

| Project Type | Method | Year | Participants |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research Project | Qualitative | 2022 | 2 regular Discord users |

**Skills Demonstrated:** Qualitative interviewing · Phenomenologically informed thematic analysis · Inductive coding · Theme development · Lifeworld analysis · Reflexivity · Research reporting

## Study Context

Social media platforms provide substantially different environments for interpersonal communication. At the time this project was undertaken, psychological research on social media had predominantly examined platforms such as Facebook and Instagram, while comparatively little attention had been given to Discord and its extensive use of synchronous voice communication.

Discord is distinctive because users can move between persistent text and voice channels while remaining physically separated and, if they choose, visually anonymous. Voice communication provides tone, timing, hesitation, and other vocal information that text alone does not, while optional video introduces a further boundary around visual and embodied presence.

This exploratory project therefore focused on what socialising through Discord felt like to regular users and what significance they gave to these interactions, rather than simply measuring frequency or duration of platform use.

## Research Question

**How do regular Discord users describe their social experiences on the platform?**

## Method

The study used an exploratory qualitative design informed by phenomenology. Two regular Discord users took part. Both reported using Discord for social interaction on approximately a daily basis and regularly participated in server voice channels.

Participants were recruited from two Discord servers of which the researcher was also a member. The researcher was acquainted with the participants before recruitment, making the researcher's insider position an important part of the methodological context.

Interviews were conducted remotely using Zoom. Participants and the interviewer joined from their respective homes using microphones and headphones; cameras were not used. Audio was recorded through Zoom, initially transcribed with Otter.ai, and then manually corrected where automated transcription was inaccurate.

Unstructured interviews were chosen to allow participants to describe experiences in their own terms and to let the interviewer follow experiences that emerged during the conversation. The project materials also describe a pilot interview that informed the use of open-ended prompts and follow-up questions, although the available records do not state unambiguously whether that pilot was separate from the final two-participant dataset.

## Analytical Approach

The analytical procedure is best described as **phenomenologically informed thematic analysis**, rather than as a pure Husserlian phenomenological analysis. Coding was inductive and proceeded from the interview material toward themes before those themes were considered through phenomenological concepts.

The workflow consisted of:

1. repeated reading of transcripts for familiarisation;
2. identification of meaningful extracts;
3. generation of initial codes;
4. refinement into final codes capturing experiential significance;
5. grouping related final codes into themes;
6. interpretation through the lifeworld dimensions of **time, space, embodiment, and intersubjectivity**; and
7. comparison of patterns across interviews.

The analysis was additionally informed by phenomenological reduction, imaginative variation, and horizontalisation. The original coding was conducted separately for each participant. For the revised report, those participant-level themes were synthesised into four cross-case themes without changing the underlying interview material.

The surviving code-development work provides an audit trail from raw extracts to initial codes, final codes, participant-level themes, and lifeworld interpretation.

## Findings

| Theme | Central experience |
|---|---|
| Social connection in conditions of isolation | Discord provided access to friendship, companionship, and social support when face-to-face interaction was limited. |
| Mediated distance, intimacy, and embodied presence | Physical distance could increase comfort and openness while removing embodied information and creating boundaries around trust and privacy. |
| Discord as social infrastructure for learning and everyday life | Social interaction supported learning, everyday functioning, motivation, and contact with diverse communities. |
| Openness and scale as sources of friction | Large, fluid communities could become overwhelming, impersonal, conflictual, and difficult to navigate. |

### Social connection in conditions of isolation

A central feature of both accounts was Discord's ability to sustain social connection when offline interaction was limited. Participant 1 described discovering Discord during a period in which they had few friends and subsequently finding friendships and support through communities on the platform.

Voice interaction also changed the experience of being physically alone. Participant 1 described the comfort of having somebody "kinda like here" through a voice channel. This suggests that auditory social presence could alter the experience of the participant's immediate physical environment even though the other person was not co-present.

Participant 2 similarly described Discord as particularly important during COVID-19 restrictions, explaining that it "helped me be socialised." They also described a shift from seeing internet friendships as unusual toward viewing them as a legitimate form of social life.

Online interaction did not entirely replace embodied contact. Participant 1 still described occasional moments of wanting in-person communication. Discord therefore functioned as meaningful social contact without eliminating the distinction between mediated and physical co-presence.

### Mediated distance, intimacy, and embodied presence

Physical distance had an ambiguous role. Participant 1 described online interaction as potentially more authentic because it reduced the awkwardness and self-consciousness associated with first meetings. They felt that relationships could become personal relatively quickly: "it just gets a lot more personal a lot quicker."

The absence of physical presence did not make embodiment irrelevant. Instead, the body took on different meanings. Participant 1 described imagining what friends looked like before seeing them and then adjusting that mental image when a photograph or camera image became available.

For Participant 1, switching on a camera could signify trust. For Participant 2, using a webcam in public servers felt uncomfortable because physical appearance was treated as personal information. The same affordance therefore represented greater intimacy for one participant and a privacy boundary for another.

Participant 2 also noted that missing facial expressions and body language could make intentions harder to interpret. Mediated distance could consequently make disclosure easier while simultaneously removing interpersonal cues that support mutual understanding.

### Discord as social infrastructure for learning and everyday life

Both participants described Discord as useful beyond social interaction as an end in itself. Participant 2 valued access to people from different cultural and linguistic backgrounds, particularly through language-learning communities. The platform enabled interaction with fluent speakers who were not readily available in the participant's local environment.

Participant 1 similarly described access to a broad range of people and communities as educational. Social interaction functioned as a route to information and learning rather than simply as a setting for conversation.

Participant 1 also linked social interaction on Discord to everyday functioning. Voice-chat interaction was described as helping with organising thoughts, completing tasks, motivation, self-care, and household activities. These are subjective accounts rather than evidence that Discord causes cognitive or clinical improvement, but they show how the participant experienced social presence as structuring activity beyond the conversation itself.

Discord also provided continuity during stressful transitions. Participant 1 described maintaining contact during a relationship breakup and while adjusting to relocation to another country, making the platform a relatively portable social environment across changes in physical location.

### Openness and scale as sources of friction

The characteristics that made Discord socially expansive could also make it difficult to navigate. Participant 2 described large public voice channels as chaotic and overwhelming, particularly when many people spoke over one another or groups changed continuously.

This fluidity could make interactions brief and difficult to remember. Entering an unfamiliar conversation could also involve passive listening until an appropriate opportunity to speak appeared. Discord could therefore lower barriers to accessing strangers while creating a different interactional challenge: deciding when and how to enter an already active social space.

Interpersonal conflict was another part of this experience. Participant 2 described political disagreements, racist or misogynistic comments, provocative behaviour, and trolling. They also acknowledged occasions when their own anger led them to troll users who had upset them.

This produces a central tension in the findings: openness increased opportunities to encounter unfamiliar people and perspectives, but the same openness could reduce continuity, increase social overload, expose users to hostile interactions, and make relationships more transient.

## Reflexivity and Ethics

The researcher's position as a regular Discord user and acquaintance of the participants is methodologically important. This insider status may have supported rapport and contextual understanding. The original project also notes that sharing the researcher's own Discord experiences sometimes encouraged participants to elaborate.

The same position may have influenced which experiences appeared significant and how conversations developed. The analysis should therefore be understood as situated and interpretative rather than as a neutral extraction of meaning from the interviews.

Participants received information about the research and provided consent before taking part. Identifying information, including usernames, server names, dates of birth, and locations, was removed from research materials. Audio recordings, transcripts, and consent documentation were stored on an encrypted drive.

The interview design deliberately avoided directly prompting highly sensitive topics such as cyberbullying and cyberostracism. This reduced the likelihood of unnecessary distress but also limited the study's ability to explore negative online experiences comprehensively.

## Strengths and Limitations

A major strength is the depth of attention given to individual experience. The unstructured interview format allowed unexpected issues to emerge, including webcam trust, imagined physical appearance, language learning, social overload, and the gamelike feel of the platform.

The code-development material is another strength because it makes the progression from extracts to codes, themes, and lifeworld interpretation visible. This improves analytical transparency.

The project nevertheless has substantial limitations. The sample consisted of only two participants and cannot be treated as representative of Discord users generally. Both participants came from communities in which the researcher was already present, so the sample reflects a narrow social network and existing relationships may have influenced disclosure.

Participant demographic information is not reported in the available study document, limiting assessment of transferability. Transcription initially relied on automated software and the report does not describe a systematic transcript-verification procedure. The pilot interview's relationship to the final dataset is also ambiguous in the surviving materials.

The original analysis generated separate themes for each participant. The revised cross-case structure improves communication of the broader conceptual relationships but does not retrospectively change that original coding process.

Finally, some experiences were closely connected to the COVID-19 pandemic. The social conditions under which the interviews took place should therefore be considered when interpreting the importance participants attributed to Discord.

## Conclusion

For these two participants, Discord was a meaningful social environment rather than simply a technological substitute for face-to-face interaction. Voice-based communication supported friendship, companionship, learning, and continuity during periods of isolation and change.

At the same time, mediated interaction changed rather than removed interpersonal constraints. Physical distance could facilitate authenticity while limiting non-verbal communication; visibility could communicate trust while threatening privacy; and access to large communities could enable discovery while producing overload and conflict.

The project therefore does not support a simple conclusion that Discord is inherently beneficial or harmful. Instead, it illustrates how the platform's communication features reorganised participants' experiences of social presence, intimacy, embodiment, community, and interpersonal friction.
